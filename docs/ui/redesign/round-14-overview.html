Round 14: Overview, made by the bot and by you

Today a bot author can only pick which of nine fixed tiles show and in what order, and you can't change anything. Three ways to make it flexible. In all three, blocks are filled from what the bot records (d.record, d.progress, d.next, steps, failures, screenshots) and from its state, and your changes are kept per bot with a way back to the bot's own layout. AYou arrange it

Customize turns the Overview into a grid of blocks: drag to move, the corner to resize, ◐ for its options (what it shows, its colour), ✕ to hide; ＋ adds a block from a gallery of kinds bound to what the bot records. The bot author only sets which blocks come first.

  • Each person makes it theirs, live.
  • Built-in kinds cover most bots.
  • The author can't add blocks of their own.

BThe bot designs it

The author lays the Overview out in overview.toml: sections of blocks, each a kind (number, ring, chart, list, text, button) bound to something the bot records or keeps. You pin, fold and reorder sections, nothing finer.

  • Every bot gets an Overview that fits it, versioned with its code.
  • Simple for people using the bot.
  • Little room for your own taste.

CBoth: the bot adds blocks, you arrange them

The author sets the default layout (overview.toml) and can add blocks of their own, from data or from code (@d.block, a kind and its values), which show in the gallery under the bot's name. You arrange, resize, hide and add any of them; ↺ goes back to the bot's layout.

  • The most flexible for both, the way plugins extend darbit elsewhere.
  • One block system for the Overview, later for other pages.
  • The most to build (blocks from code run in the bot and reach the Studio through the service).
